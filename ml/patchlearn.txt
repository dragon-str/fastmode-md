analysis:
The learned proposal does not beat the random baseline. Its per-water cost is
about 6.5 kT at n=1 (200 proposals, seed 11) against the baseline 0.9 kT, and the
joint slope is 11.6 kT/water against the baseline 1.87. An earlier 40-proposal
run showed 0.026 kT at n=1; that was seed noise, not a real gain.

Why it loses:
1. The conditional model is informative. Predicting the water dipole in the box
   frame from the local descriptor reaches R2 0.75 (residual std about 0.3 on a
   unit vector). A local frame built from three neighbour oxygens was unstable and
   dropped R2 to 0.13; the absolute frame is required.
2. The conditional spread is large. Sampling from p(orientation | context)
   proposes a large rotation, near 20 degrees. The baseline rotates by 0.10 rad,
   about 6 degrees. The learned move is simply bigger, so dU is bigger.
3. Independent patch draws are not a joint sample. For n>1 each water is drawn
   from its own conditional, so the patch breaks the mutual hydrogen-bond network.
   This is the patch-internal correlation that sets the phase2 slope.

What a winning proposal needs:
- A joint (correlated) proposal, such as an autoregressive or flow model trained
  to match the sampling context, not independent per-water draws.
- A fair efficiency metric, not raw acceptance: acceptance times mean squared
  move, or acceptance at matched step size.
- An equivariant model; the absolute-frame trick only works for one fixed box.
